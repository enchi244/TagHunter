Can you get a discount on damaged items at TJ Maxx?

By Tag Hunter · Based on the Clearance Hunter Handbook · Updated 25 September 2026

Sometimes. Associates often have some latitude to reduce an item with a minor flaw. Nobody is obliged to say yes, and how you ask changes the answer more than what is wrong with the item.

Check the ticket first

If the item is already ticketed “as is,” the reduction has already been taken. Don't ask again.

What kind of flaw

Think minor and cosmetic: a missing or loose button, a chipped rim on a ceramic piece, a dented box on packaged goods. Whether it counts is the associate's call.

How to ask

Point at the flaw, ask a real question, and then stop talking. If the answer is no, say thanks and decide whether you still want it at the ticket price. Be genuinely fine with a no: pushing after a refusal costs you goodwill in a store you want to keep shopping in.

The handbook has the exact wording and what makes a yes more likely on page 22.

Keep it in perspective

This is a bonus, not a strategy. It is worth a few extra dollars on items you were buying anyway. The real money is in reading the tags and timing your visits: see our tag colors guide and markdown schedule guide.

What is confirmed and what isn't

TJ Maxx has no published policy that promises a damage discount. This reflects how shoppers and associates describe it, and it will differ from store to store. This guide is written for TJ Maxx stores in the United States.

Questions

Does TJ Maxx give discounts on damaged items?

Sometimes. Associates often have some latitude to reduce an item with a minor flaw, but nobody is obliged to say yes, and it varies by store and by person.

What if the item is already tagged as is?

Check first. If the item is already ticketed as is, the reduction has already been taken, so do not ask again.

What kind of damage is worth mentioning?

Minor flaws such as a missing or loose button, a small snag or open seam, or a chipped rim on ceramics. What counts is up to the associate.

Is it rude to ask for a discount on a damaged item?

It is a normal question if you ask politely and are genuinely fine with a no. Pushing after a refusal costs you goodwill in a store you want to keep shopping in.

Is asking for a damage discount worth the effort?

It is a bonus, not a strategy. It is worth a few extra dollars on items you were buying anyway. Timing and tag-reading are where the real money is.
