What do TJ Maxx tag colors mean?

By Tag Hunter · Based on the Clearance Hunter Handbook · Updated 25 September 2026

There are two signals on every item. The color of the printed ticket tells you what kind of item you're holding. The color of the sticker on top tells you where it sits in the markdown cycle. Read both and you know whether to buy now or wait.

White says wait. Red says maybe. Yellow says now.

The colors at a glance

ColorWhat it means, and your move
WhiteRegular off-price ticket. Nothing has been discounted yet. Wait.
BluePart of a coordinated set. Find the match first.
PurpleThe Runway, the designer department. Look closer.
RedMarked down at least once, and likely to fall again. Wait, or maybe.
Red ×2+Stickers stacked on top of each other. You're near the floor. Lean buy.
YellowFinal markdown. The bottom of the cycle. Buy now.

Signal one: the color of the ticket

The printed ticket is attached to the item from the start. Its color says what the item is, not how far it has been marked down.

$49.99Ticket

White: regular price

This is the everyday off-price number. Nothing has been discounted yet, so in most cases the smart move is to wait. The exception is a size or a piece you know you won't find again.

$29.99Ticket

Blue: coordinates

A blue ticket means the item is part of a matching set. The partner piece is usually racked nearby, but the pieces sell separately and may not be marked the same. Find the match before you commit.

$89.99Ticket

Purple: the Runway

Purple is TJ Maxx's elevated designer department. The ticket starts higher, and the department is stocked in selected stores only. The handbook's view: purple plus a red sticker is the single best value signal in the store.

Signal two: the color of the sticker

Stickers go on top of the ticket as an item is marked down. This is the signal most shoppers misread, because a sticker is not the finish line.

$34.99Sticker

One red sticker: a checkpoint

The item has been marked down at least once. Red appears all year round, and a red-stickered item is still moving through the cycle, so another reduction is likely if it doesn't sell. Waiting is usually right unless it's a hard-to-find size. If you're undecided, red is the color you're allowed to walk away from.

$34.99$24.99Sticker

Two or three red stickers: near the floor

Stickers stacked on top of each other mean several reductions. That's stubborn stock, common with seasonal clothing and holiday decor, and you're close to the bottom. It usually means nobody wanted it, which isn't the same as it being bad: odd sizes and off-season colors get punished hardest. Lean toward buying.

$14.99Sticker

Yellow: final clearance

Yellow is the final markdown, intended to be the lowest price the item will carry. Unsold yellow stock eventually leaves the floor, either sold on to a third party or donated. If you want it and it fits, this is the moment. There is no later. More on yellow stickers.

Two catches worth knowing

Yellow is meant to be final, but the big January and July clearance events can be an exception. And there is a red-sticker trap worth checking for before you trust a markdown. Both are covered in the handbook on pages 8 and 9, with anything we couldn't confirm labeled as such.

What colors can't tell you

  • When your store marks down. There is no national markdown day. Schedules are set store by store, so any pattern you hear is a starting hypothesis for your store, not a rule. See the markdown schedule guide.
  • Whether the price is good. A sticker tells you an item was marked down, not that it's worth buying. Buy the item, never the percentage: if you wouldn't want it at the shelf price with no comparison shown, the discount isn't a saving. See our Compare At guide.

What is confirmed and what isn't

TJX has never published its internal tagging logic. Parts of what shoppers “know” come from former employees and viral videos, and some of it contradicts itself, so this guide labels anything that isn't solid. It's written for TJ Maxx stores in the United States. Marshalls and HomeGoods are sister banners that run the same January and July events on their own timelines, but their tag systems aren't confirmed to match.

Questions

What does a red sticker mean at TJ Maxx?

The item has been marked down at least once. Red appears all year round, and a red-stickered item is still moving through the markdown cycle, so another reduction is likely if it doesn't sell. If you're undecided, red is the color you're allowed to walk away from.

What does a yellow sticker mean at TJ Maxx?

Yellow is the final markdown: the lowest price the item is intended to carry. Unsold yellow stock eventually leaves the floor, either sold on or donated, so if you want it, that is the moment to buy.

What does a purple tag mean at TJ Maxx?

Purple marks the Runway, TJ Maxx's elevated designer department. Prices start higher, and it is stocked in selected stores only. The handbook calls a purple ticket with a red sticker the best value signal in the store.

What does a blue tag mean at TJ Maxx?

Blue means the item is part of a coordinated set. The matching piece is usually racked nearby, but the pieces sell separately, so find the match before you commit.

Do these colors work the same at Marshalls and HomeGoods?

Not confirmed. Marshalls and HomeGoods are TJX sister banners and run the same January and July events on their own timelines, but their tag systems are not confirmed to match TJ Maxx's. Check your own store.

Is there a day when TJ Maxx marks everything down?

No. There is no national markdown day. Markdown schedules are set store by store, so the way to find yours is to observe your own store for a few weeks.
